Method & Sources

How the numbers are made

Where near the lunar south pole, and on which dates, can a lander keep its solar panels in sunlight and its antenna in sight of Earth?

Method

  1. Terrain. Around each candidate site, sample the lunar elevation model (LRO LOLA polar terrain and the NASA ADC Artemis III region files).
  2. 360° horizon. From the site, step outward along each of 360 one-degree azimuths and keep the highest elevation angle. The result is the site's horizon array.
  3. Sun and Earth. Hourly apparent azimuth and elevation of the Sun and Earth at the site from JPL Horizons, using the site as a topocentric observer on the Moon.
  4. Checks. Sunlit when the Sun's disk clears the horizon at its azimuth, with the visible fraction of the disk recorded as sunFrac. Earth link when Earth's centre is above the horizon at its azimuth.
  5. Metrics. Percent of hours sunlit, longest continuous darkness, percent of hours with an Earth link, comms windows, and a simple battery model: charge × visible disk when lit, a fixed drain when dark.

NASA comparison

When a site has nasaIllumPct, the site sheet shows our percent sunlit next to NASA's average illumination value for the same point (LOLA-derived polar illumination maps). A large gap means the horizon, the time span or the coordinates need checking. Our window is 60 days, while NASA's maps average over many years, so some gap is expected.

Limitations

  • Terrain extent. Distant peaks beyond the sampled area are missing, which makes horizons look too low and sunlight too generous.
  • Resolution. One horizon value per degree and per-hour time steps can miss short shadows and narrow notches.
  • Exaggeration. The 3D horizon ring and the hardware model scale heights and elevations by 10 so differences of a degree are visible. Only the readouts are true values.
  • Earth libration. Earth's wobble in the lunar sky is approximated. Earth is treated as a point at its centre, without parallax.
  • Battery. A teaching model with editable charge and drain rates, not a lander power budget.
  • Coordinates. Candidate-region coordinates are approximate and only represent each region with a single point.

Sources

  • JPL Horizons: Sun and Earth ephemerides.
  • LRO LOLA terrain data and NASA ADC Artemis III candidate region files: elevation and horizons.
  • LROC Quickmap: site inspection.
  • NASA Moon Trek: site inspection.
  • NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit: LROC colour map and LOLA height map on the globe.
  • Three.js: 3D rendering. Type: Playfair Display and Inter (Google Fonts).

The model

A tabletop Moon

An Arduino drives a turntable and two servo arms carrying a Sun LED and an Earth LED around a 3D-printed site. Sensors on the lander report whether the light and the Earth signal actually reach it, and the app checks that against its prediction.
